AQUAQUILL: Filling the Wallacean Shortfall for the Aquatic Quillworts

Improving knowledge of the distribution, populations and conservation needs of selected aquatic quillworts in Greece.

← Back to all projects
Funding National Environment & Climate Change Agency (N.E.C.C.A.)
Duration2025–2026
Principal InvestigatorKonstantinos Stefanidis

Project overview

Freshwater plants remain under-recorded in many regions, limiting our understanding of changes in their distribution and conservation status. This knowledge gap is particularly evident for Isoetes (quillworts), a distinctive and ancient lineage of aquatic and semi-aquatic lycopods whose small, grass-like appearance can make them easy to overlook during field surveys.

In Greece, the distribution of several Isoetes species is still incompletely known. AQUAQUILL addresses this knowledge gap, with particular emphasis on the two Greek endemic quillworts, I. heldreichii and I. haussknechtii. The project aims to improve information on their occurrence, population status, habitat conditions and potential threats, while also contributing DNA barcode data for selected Greek species. AQUAQUILL is implemented in the framework of H.F.R.I.’s 2nd Call “Actions to protect, conserve and promote biodiversity. Field studies of endemic, endangered and nationally important species of Greece” funded by the National Environment & Climate Change Agency (N.E.C.C.A.)

Target species

The project focuses on four species for which additional field information and/or DNA barcode data are needed:

Isoetes heldreichii

Greek endemic historically recorded near Palaiokastro, Thessaly. It has not been recorded since its original description and is assessed as Critically Endangered (Possibly Extinct). Surveys are needed to investigate whether it persists at nearby freshwater habitats.

Isoetes haussknechtii

Greek endemic known from localities in Thessaly, Central Peloponnese and Lesvos. Its population trend is not yet known, highlighting the need to assess its populations, local abundance and habitat conditions.

Isoetes phrygia

A range-restricted species recorded in Türkiye and at a small number of localities in Crete. The project includes targeted sampling to obtain material for DNA barcoding.

Isoetes todaroana

Previously considered endemic to Italy and more recently recorded in western Greece. Targeted field visits will collect specimens to contribute barcode data for this species.

Research objectives

  • Search for I. heldreichii and I. haussknechtii in freshwater ecosystems of Thessaly.
  • Assess known populations and habitats of I. haussknechtii in Central Peloponnese and Lesvos.
  • Collect selected Isoetes specimens and generate DNA barcodes to improve publicly available molecular reference databases.

Study areas & fieldwork

Fieldwork spans representative river networks, lakes, and transitional water bodies across Greece. Systematic seasonal sampling is carried out to record aquatic flora, water quality parameters, and hydromorphological features.

Methods

The project combines targeted field surveys, habitat assessment, specimen identification and DNA barcoding. Fieldwork is planned across suitable seasons, taking into account the aquatic or semi-aquatic habitats and seasonal moisture requirements of quillworts. Specimens collected during the surveys will be examined and selected material will be submitted for DNA barcode analysis at certified laboratories.

Expected scientific and conservation contribution

AQUAQUILL is expected to improve knowledge of the distribution and population status of Greek quillworts, particularly the two endemic species. New records and population information can support future reassessments of their conservation status. Data on habitat conditions and potential pressures will help identify conservation needs, while DNA barcodes will contribute to filling gaps in molecular reference databases for Greek Isoetes.

Results

Main Findings

  • Habitats have declined due to extensive drought conditions, overgrazing and other anthropogenic activities.
  • Isoetes haussknechtii, a Greek endemic, was found at one location on Lesvos, almost 20 years after its latest record. Specimens were collected and molecular analysis was conducted for DNA barcode deposition.
  • Isoetes heldreichii was not found and is presumed extinct. However, additional sampling in mountainous wetlands of Western Greece may reveal previously undetected populations.
  • A large population of Isoetes phrygia persists in Lake Omalos, Crete. The lake retains some water even during harsh summer conditions, allowing the population to survive. It is also the location of the newly described Greek endemic Ranunculus dahlgreniae.
  • Elodea canadensis was also recorded in summer 2025, representing a new record of this species in Greece.
  • Habitats of Isoetes todaroana at coastal locations in western Greece have been heavily degraded, and the species was not found.
